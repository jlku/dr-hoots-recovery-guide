Discharge instructions following cochlear implantation

You had surgery today to place a cochlear implant on the right side. The bulky head wrap over your ear keeps pressure on the incision and should stay on until the day after tomorrow, when you may take it off at home and throw it away. Underneath you will find a line of surgical tape over the incision; leave the tape alone and let it fall off on its own over the next week or two. Keep the area dry. You may take a shower and wash your hair three days from now, but do not let the shower spray hit the ear directly and do not soak or swim. Expect the ear to feel numb, full, and swollen, and expect some popping or clicking sounds; this is normal and settles over several weeks. You may feel dizzy for the first few days. Get up slowly and do not drive until the dizziness has passed. We did not send you home on an antibiotic. For pain, take acetaminophen 650 mg every six hours as needed, and please avoid ibuprofen, naproxen, aspirin and other anti-inflammatories for the first ten days because they increase the risk of bleeding. Do not lift anything heavier than ten pounds and do not blow your nose for two weeks. Call the office at 617-555-0142 if you develop a fever over 101.5, increasing redness or swelling around the incision, drainage from the wound, or a severe headache. Our nurse will call you tomorrow. We will see you back in the office in about two weeks for a wound check, and your device will be turned on by the audiologist roughly four weeks after surgery.
