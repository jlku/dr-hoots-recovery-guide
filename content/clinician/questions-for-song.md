## What the provider page needs before it is designed any further

Three days of design work keep arriving at these two, and neither is an engineering problem. Both are cheap to answer and both decide what gets built next.

- `q.medication-varies` (open, asked 2026-09-20, John's read 2026-09-20: "the summaries vary little per patient" — still needs Song, who is the one who writes them; segment 2 conditional beats; `guide/provider.html`) — For a routine implant, do the antibiotic and pain-medicine answers change from patient to patient, or are they how your practice works — "I send everyone home on cephalexin", "I have stopped writing opioids for these"? They are the only two things in the whole guide that change what a patient is *told*: everything else is the same for everyone, language only picks which recording plays, and the follow-up date is text on a card that is never spoken. If they are practice-level they belong in a setup done once, and the screen a nurse uses thirty times a week has a date on it and nothing else. If they vary per patient, the page must ask every time and ask well, because getting it wrong is how a patient hears "take your antibiotic the way it was prescribed" when they were never given one. We built the second way without asking. *When answered:* either the provider page loses most of its controls, or the per-patient questions stay and get designed properly.
- `q.real-avs-documents` (open, asked 2026-09-20; `guide/assets/provider.js`, `qa/import/`) — Please send six real after-visit summaries as they come out of the chart, identifiers removed: the actual text, however messy, not a description. The page reads a clinician's instructions and works out which parts the guide can carry, and it has only ever been tested against the draft dot phrase in this repository, which we wrote ourselves. Pointed at six notes written to *look* realistic, the parser recognised none of the values in any of them. That failure is real and its worst consequence is fixed, but those six notes were invented, so we still do not know the thing that decides how to fix it properly: what shape your instructions are actually in. A numbered list, a paragraph of prose, an Epic SmartPhrase with pick-lists and a page pasted out of a PDF need genuinely different handling. *When answered:* `qa/import/` is replaced with real shapes and the parser is written against them instead of against our own document.
- `q.presets-approved` (open, asked 2026-09-20; `content/provider/presets.json`) — The page's defaults for a missing instruction are antibiotic yes, pain medicine yes, English, no follow-up date. Nobody has approved them and the file has always said so. Until this week the page applied them anyway and in silence: a discharge note whose own words were "We did not send you home on an antibiotic" produced a link that told the patient to take one. Now an unapproved default is offered by name and never applied by itself, and no link is built for a value nobody authored — so the page asks where it used to guess. Confirm these four, or give us the right ones. *When answered:* `approved` becomes true, and a block that leaves a line out stops being a question.

**What John's read implies, if Song confirms it.** If the summary barely changes between patients, then
the antibiotic and pain-medicine answers barely change either, and they are practice constants rather than
patient variables. Two of the five driving fields stop being per-patient questions: they get set once for
the practice, in the same act as answering `q.presets-approved`. What genuinely varies per patient is the
follow-up date and which language the patient reads. That is the whole per-patient screen, and it is why
the current one — sixteen controls and twelve regions — is mostly collecting answers that do not change.

## How we judge the pictures, for your sign-off

- `q.instruction-picture-rule` (open, asked 2026-09-18; rule in `reviewers/anatomy/adjudicator.md`, enforced by `scripts/lib/adjudication.mjs`) — Three reviewers who see only the picture must each say what it tells them to do, and all three must get it right. We also make each of them name one way a worried patient could misread it. For an action that can run backwards, such as taking a bandage off, they always name the reverse ("it could be putting it on"), even when they themselves read it correctly. Our rule: that forced answer alone does not fail the picture. It fails only when two or more reviewers name the same misreading and it involves harm or the wrong body part. That rule has already caught gauze that read as cloth at the throat and a dashed line that read as "cut here". John approved this for now on 2026-09-18. Is it acceptable to you, or do you want any named misreading to fail a picture? *When answered:* the rule is confirmed or tightened, and the two pictures it passed are re-judged.
- `q.state-picture-misreadings` (open, asked 2026-09-18; rule in `reviewers/anatomy/adjudicator.md`) — Each of three reviewers is also asked what a worried patient might mistake a picture for. On pictures that show a state, such as the healed incision or the worn processor, they always name something alarming: a fresh cut, an eye patch, an ordinary hearing aid with a stuck-on patch. Today any one of those that matches a forbidden reading fails the picture, and in the first live test that failed every accepted state picture. In effect now as a placeholder, approved by John on 2026-09-18: these imagined misreadings reach you as notes on every picture instead of failing it, while anything a reviewer reports actually seeing still fails it. Instruction cards keep the stricter rule, because a viewer might act on a misreading there. Is that acceptable, or should a misreading named by two or more reviewers still fail a picture that only shows a body part? *When answered:* the rule for state pictures is set and the calibration is run again.
